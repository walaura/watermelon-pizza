## Work

![its all so so corny lol](./pix/hey-chat/twt.png?as=webp)

Around 18 months ago I made an a now weirdly prophetic post about getting promoted as well as [gaining a cool scepter](https://bsky.app/profile/freezydorito.lol/post/3lhwbzwhibc25) and then went radio silent for a while. 

The scepter was cursed. Go figure.

At the time I took the role, growing a design systems team from the front felt like a dream come true, it knocked out all the boxes. I loved digital design and working out how to scale it throu systems was a passion of min, and was a bit of an expert in the area got talks on this and everything. I was super ready for my next career leap and genuinely excited to tackle this challenge.

There's a lot of specifics I must leave out but yeah the fucking scepter was cursed. After a couple months of powering through what I initially chalked as a small initial disillusionment I ended up stressed and burned out and eventually decided to sever ties with Meta. 

## Career

At the same time this was all happening, the rules for the entire field of software engineering is being rewritten. I have very much missed out on this whole AI thing which honestly sounds like a blessing. 

I think a lot (plenty of time to think) about how much of a boomer this makes me but i honestly just don't have a lot of interest for the new ways we build software. The tech is fire IMO, it owns that we have given GPUs anxiety. The way capital is bringing these tools to the workers is, well, less cool. 

Every potential employer I have talked to is way too excited about finally turning software from manual work into an assembly line and this time, unlike when they tried to rally us around a dumb trello board every morning, this time they got the machinery for it and they are wasting no time in bringing it to every factory.

It's all very existential of course, I won't bore you with criticisms of AI, it's pretty awful. When it comes to my field specifically, to frontend dev, I'm fairly disullusioned because AI output is good enough. Having worked with enough humans as I have it's definitely above average.

The work already moved from making cool brochure sites when I started to wrangling virtual machines to run node in so your css will compile for whatever fucked up techbro reason. We really lost the plot after Flash fucks sake.

Trying to look a little forward in what's next - no reason to believe any of this will slow down - hot assembly line shit shit like optimizing processes and workflows and pipelines for code generation generally sounds like as whole lot of the unfun part with none of the fun parts.

Less hot assembly line shit like sitting in front of the conveyor belt watching 200 prs pass by you a day and pushing a little red button to take it off the line when one is wrong (or not, who gives a shit lol) also sounds as fun as watching paint dry
